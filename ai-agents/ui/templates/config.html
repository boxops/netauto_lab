🤖 Autonomy Policies
What are autonomy policies?
Policies serve two roles. First, they set the autonomy level (L0–L5) — how much independence the AI has when proposing and executing a fix (L2 requires your approval, L4 auto-executes after meeting confidence + prior-success thresholds). Second, policies marked with ⚡ carry programmatic conditions: live checks (Prometheus metrics, device show commands, or Nautobot fields) that resolve a well-understood alert in seconds with zero LLM calls before the AI investigation even starts. If every condition passes, the RCA and fix are generated from templates; if any condition fails the pipeline falls back to the normal AI path automatically. The Learning Engine automatically promotes or demotes the autonomy level based on whether past fixes actually resolved the alert — so the system earns autonomy incrementally rather than being granted it upfront.

🎯 Standing Intents
What are standing intents?
Intents are persistent declarations about how the network should behave. Unlike policies (which control how much autonomy the AI uses), intents control when the AI acts and what it does with specific alert types.
